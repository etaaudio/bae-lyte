# BAE and BAE Lyte privacy policy

Last updated: November 27, 2026

BAE (B-audio Enhancer) and BAE Lyte are browser extensions published by ETA Audio LLC. This policy covers both. BAE Lyte does a subset of what BAE does, so everything below applies to it too, except the parts about features it doesn't have.

## What they collect

**Nothing.** Neither extension collects, store, transmit or sell any personal information or browsing data.

## What they store

Your own settings (in BAE: codec choice, Purist and normalization options, playback speed, pitch correction, crossfeed, B-Mode, clip guard, output device and display options; in BAE Lyte: Purist and codec choice) are saved in your browser's local extension storage so they persist between sessions. They stay on your device. Removing the extension removes them.

## Network requests

Neither extension sends anything anywhere. There are no analytics, no advertising and no third-party code.

To show the sample rate and bit depth of what is playing, an extension may request the first part of the audio file or stream playlist the page is already playing, from the same address the page uses. Nothing about you is added to that request, and what comes back is only read for its format and then discarded.

## Sites they run on

Both extensions run on every website, so their settings work in any tab that plays audio or video. Your browser shows this permission before you install. On each page they read which audio and video formats the player asks for and uses, and adjust its volume (and in BAE, its speed, audio processing and output device). They do not read any other page content.

## Microphone prompt (BAE only)

If you use "Show my output devices", your browser asks for microphone access on that site, because browsers only list audio outputs for sites allowed to use the microphone. BAE switches the microphone off again immediately and never records or listens.
